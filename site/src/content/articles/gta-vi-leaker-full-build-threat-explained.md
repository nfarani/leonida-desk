The group behind the Grand Theft Auto VI gameplay leaks has reportedly posted an ultimatum: if its cryptocurrency reaches a $30 million market cap, "the build goes out", with the full story mode played to the end.[^c49] Nothing has been released, and outlets do not agree on what would be.[^c51][^c64]

*This is an unverified threat from an anonymous source. We are not linking to the leaker's site, the footage or the coin, and we are not describing story content from the leaked videos.*

## What the message says

The message offers the publisher a single exit: "Take-Two gets one way out: physical disks and a public apology."[^c50] That fits the group's stated motive, which is a protest against the game being sold without a physical version, although the same group has been promoting a crypto scheme throughout.[^c65]

Lower targets on the same coin were tied to footage of Lucia, one of the two lead characters, wearing less.[^c54]

## What has been verified, and by whom

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The leaker returned this week with a long gameplay video | Reported by IGN, which puts it at about 24 minutes[^c55] |
| That video is real | Eurogamer says it has seen it and that it appears legitimate[^c56] |
| The leaker holds a complete, playable game | Claimed by the leaker only[^c49] |
| What "the build" means | Disputed: IGN says it could be a build or a filmed playthrough; VGC reads it as a working build[^c51][^c64] |
| The target will be reached | IGN calls it unlikely[^c52] |

## The money

The target is far from where the coin stands. IGN recorded a market cap of $3.8 million when it published, after a peak of $5.7 million earlier the same day.[^c52] Polygon, writing a day later, put it at $6 million and rising.[^c53]

There is a history here. Kotaku reports that the account appeared to withdraw about $250,000 of its own coin in late August, and that the coin then crashed.[^c60] IGN's figure for the same episode is around $270,000.[^c61] Kotaku's reading of the new offer is blunt: it looks like a second round of a rug pull.[^c62]

## What Take-Two and Rockstar have done

Take-Two subpoenaed Discord and Microsoft in August for user records connected to the videos, and there has been no public word on what came back.[^c59] VGC adds that a separate request to YouTube was withdrawn, with the publisher telling the court that "Take-Two has determined it no longer needs the particular information requested".[^c66]

Rockstar's own position was set out earlier in the saga, when it called the leaks heartbreaking and said "this is obviously not how we intended for you to see the game after all this time".[^c57] The game is still due on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S on 19 November.[^c58]

## Our read

The structure of the offer rewards one party whatever happens. The condition for the release is not a decision by the publisher but a price target on an asset the leaker created, and the last time that asset rose, Kotaku reports, the account behind it cashed out.[^c60] A reader does not have to settle whether the group really cares about physical media to see who benefits from a rush of buyers.[^c65]

The demand itself is also hard to take at face value. Polygon considers it highly unlikely that the publisher would agree this close to launch.[^c67] A physical edition is a manufacturing and distribution decision, and the game is weeks away.[^c58]

## What we don't know

Who is behind the account remains unknown; Kotaku says nothing more has emerged about who operates it or who they work with.[^c63] It is equally unknown how the footage left the studio, whether a complete build exists outside it, and whether the subpoenas have produced a name.[^c59]

We will update this article if Take-Two or Rockstar responds, or if the facts change.
