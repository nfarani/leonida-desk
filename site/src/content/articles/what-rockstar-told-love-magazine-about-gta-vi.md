Rockstar Games staff have given a long interview to Love Magazine about Grand Theft Auto VI, and five outlets have now reported different parts of it.[^c14][^c19][^c22][^c25][^c29] None of them has published the interview in full, so what follows is assembled from those reports, with each line traced to the outlet that carried it.

*This article relies on reports of the interview. We have not read the magazine itself, which is why it is labelled a high-confidence report and not confirmed.*

## What was said

**On politics.** Rupert Humphries, described by VGC as Rockstar's senior vice president of narrative, said the series has rarely dealt with national politics: "If you go back to the previous GTAs, there's never really been much focus on national politics".[^c14][^c15] He said the studio avoids naming real people, events or movements.[^c16] VGC adds that, by his account, players will still come across election signs, campaign robocalls and political talk radio.[^c17]

**On satire.** Humphries also described the tone: "There is a big focus on not just holding up a mirror, but on making everything satirical, larger than life, and more grotesque."[^c18]

**On realism.** Rob Nelson, described by GamesRadar+ as co-studio head of Rockstar North, said realism is a tool and not the goal.[^c19][^c20] In his words, "sometimes it's better to ignore or omit certain aspects of reality because they just don't make sense with the experience we are trying to create".[^c21]

**On the world's characters.** James Horton, described by Polygon as writing director, said: "In a way, there's no 'background' in this game".[^c26] He went on: "There's just foreground you're not paying attention to right now."[^c27]

**On research.** Aaron Garbut, described by IGN as head of development, said an early trip ran from Miami through the Florida Keys to Key West.[^c22][^c23] He also said: "We travel around with cops and with gang members."[^c24]

**On the protagonists.** PC Gamer quotes game writer Elizabeth Gauvey-Kern: "We wanted both protagonists to be vulnerable, tough, flawed, right sometimes, wrong sometimes, able to learn from each other".[^c30]

## Where the reports disagree

The outlets do not agree on one name. VGC and PC Gamer call the senior vice president of narrative Rupert Humphries; Polygon calls him Robert Humphries.[^c14][^c29][^c28] Job titles differ as well: IGN gives head of development to Garbut, while PC Gamer gives that title, alongside co-studio head, to Nelson.[^c22][^c29]

Polygon notes that its account draws on parts of the interview and screenshots shared by fans online.[^c25]

## Why it matters

Read together, the comments describe one design position from several directions. The game is meant to feel like the present without pinning itself to named people or passing trends, and its realism stops where it would become a chore. Those are choices about tone and pacing, and they are the kind of thing a trailer cannot show.

The headline phrase about a game with no background characters deserves care. In the quote as carried, it is a way of describing how much attention the writers gave to passers-by. It is not a description of a feature, and it should not be repeated as one.

## What we don't know

We do not know the full wording or order of the interview, because we have only seen what other outlets chose to quote. We also cannot settle the disputed first name or the job titles without the magazine in hand. Until then, treat every quotation above as a report of what was printed.
