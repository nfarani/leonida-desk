## Editorial angle

Coverage of the Love Magazine interview has mostly quoted the line and moved on. This short makes the line visible: it treats 'no background' as a camera idea, so the viewer watches a crowd stop being wallpaper one person at a time. It then adds the two supporting details most write-ups split across separate articles (the Florida research behind how pedestrians talk and dress, and the 'hundred different conversations' quote), and it ends by saying plainly that this is a claim from an interview that nobody has been able to check yet.

## What the viewer sees

- **Visual premise:** A busy street where everyone but one person is a blur, exactly how a game crowd normally works.
- **Visual escalation:** The focus keeps moving. Each time it lands on a new passer-by, that person turns out to be mid-story: an argument, a phone call, a distinct look. The camera speeds up until it is sprinting through overlapping conversations.
- **Visual payoff:** One stranger turns and fills the frame, then the whole street snaps into focus at once: no blur left, nobody is an extra.

## Editing notes

The whole edit is one idea: focus. Blur means 'background', sharp means 'someone'. Never cut to a shot where everything is sharp until shot 7, or the payoff is spent. Hook text is on screen from frame one, before the voice. Shot 2 is a freeze of shot 1, not a new image. Cut shot 3 so the focus lands on the word 'foreground'. Shots 5 and 6 are the escalation: let 5 run fast and cut on the whip into 6 so the stop feels sudden. Keep the small 'AI-generated visuals, not game footage' label in a corner for the full runtime. Burned-in captions, two lines maximum, quote marks only around Horton's exact words. No game art, no trailer frames, no logos anywhere.

Open cold on street ambience, no music for the first three seconds. Under shots 3 and 4 a light, warm, mid-tempo bed (licensed library track, no vocals).
